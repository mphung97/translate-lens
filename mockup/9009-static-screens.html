The Quiet Art of Arranging Type

Article · Typography · 3 min read

Typography is the art and technique of arranging type to make written language legible, readable, and appealing when displayed. The arrangement of type involves selecting typefaces, point sizes, line lengths, line-spacing, and letter-spacing, as well as adjusting the space between pairs of letters.

Well-set type is almost invisible — it carries the reader forward without drawing attention to itself. Badly set type, by contrast, whispers in every sentence, nudging the reader out of the text and back into the mechanics of the page.

Design notes

Monospace by default — the 9009 theme was born inside a typing test, so the lens speaks the same quiet, keystroke-heavy language. JetBrains Mono for text, Space Grotesk for UI.

One accent does all the work. The caret green marks detection, the active mode and the primary action; everything else stays paper and ink.

Glass → paper. The feature area is transparent while you aim, then turns opaque the instant it locks onto text. That state change is the feature.

The toolbar is the app — grip, always-on-top, mode switch, settings and close are identical on macOS and Windows. No chrome beyond what the popup needs.
